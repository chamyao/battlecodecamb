This run explores the LOCKDOWN style, which none of our policies play yet (three of the top
ladder teams do). Altars are shared by both teams; an altar lock (buy_altar_lock(), 10 gold,
on spawn; one carried at a time; has_altar_lock() tells you) placed with place_altar_lock(tile)
within 2 tiles of an altar blocks the ENEMY's tributes there (and cancels a channel in
progress) while our own team can still tribute at it. Five basic attacks clear a lock.
Develop a policy whose plan is to deny the enemy altars:
- buy a lock whenever on spawn with spare gold and not carrying one;
- place it on the altars the enemy is likely to use (closest to spawn_region(enemy()), or where
  enemy carriers are seen), and re-lock altars whose lock was broken (an altar not in
  locked_altars() that you can see);
- guard: a champion near a locked altar attacks enemies trying to clear it, and enemies who
  come to tribute carry gems (unit_gems) that drop to their killer;
- keep enough camp farming and tributing that our own score grows (we tribute at locked altars
  freely, so prefer altars we have locked).
Decide how many champions lock/guard versus farm, and when (e.g. by round, gold, team_score
lead or deficit) — name those as constants. Every version must keep buying and placing locks;
a version that drops the style is rejected.
Camp choice matters in every style, as a direction, not a rule: do NOT add a skip test on
group_threat/group_hp (measured: it loses 5-25 points). Instead, prefer a rich group (closest of
the goals intersected with rich_spawns(15), i.e. 15+ gems) over the nearest camp when it is only
a few tiles farther, and otherwise keep the nearest-camp choice that works.
